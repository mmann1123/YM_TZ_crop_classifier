# Project-Centric Literature Synthesis: Lite Learning, Efficient Crop Classification in Tanzania

This synthesis reads across every per-paper briefer in `writeup/literature/` and organizes the findings around our study's modeling choices, evaluation bar, and action items — *not* paper by paper. The single most important lens is the **evaluation protocol**: papers are only comparable when they share a protocol, so every reported number is discounted by its protocol tier and no number is quoted without naming the tier that produced it (rule 1).

Our study (`mann2025lite` below) sits at **Tier 2** — field-disjoint grouped cross-validation (`StratifiedGroupKFold` on `field_id`, `Field_size`-weighted) within a single region (Arusha, Dodoma, Mwanza), single year (2023), single sensor (Sentinel-2 optical). Headline: **Cohen's Kappa 0.82, F1-micro 0.85** out-of-field. This is NOT a spatial holdout, cross-year, or cross-sensor test.

## Section 1 — Evaluation-protocol tiers (the master lens)

Every paper is sorted by how it splits train and test, because that determines whether its numbers can be compared to ours. **Tier 1 clears a higher bar than we do** (true spatial / cross-year / cross-sensor transfer, or design-based probability sampling). **Tier 2 is our own rung** (field-wise FID grouped k-fold within one region). **Tier 3 is pooled/random splitting** (spatial-autocorrelation / field leakage — discount heavily). **Tier 4 is reviews, theory, biophysics, datasets, and context** (no comparable protocol). Within-tier ordering reflects on-point relevance to our study.

### Tier 1 — ABOVE our bar (true transfer or probability sampling)

- **`jin2019smallholder` (the strongest same-country comparator).** Tanzania + Kenya smallholder maize, classical Random Forest in GEE, deliberately chosen over deep learning. It spans three protocols, all at or above our bar: maize mapping used a **field-disjoint random-split-by-field** (= our exact Tier-2 bar); cropland mapping added a **district-based spatially disjoint holdout** ("test folds geographically distinct from training folds," = Tier 1); and yield was validated against **fully independent crop-cut ground measurements** aggregated to district level (= Tier 1). It does not commit the in-region-random-split-as-OOS conflation. Candid maize accuracies: **TZ 79%, Kenya 63% (field-disjoint)**; cropland **~85% (`S1`+`S2`, district holdout)**.
- **`pankajakshan2025deep` (best external support for the lite-learning thesis, but harder evaluation than ours).** A 2-parameter, GPU-free PCA+SVM+spatial-refinement HSI pipeline transfers cross-scene (Botswana, KSC) and cross-sensor/cross-region (Indian Pines USA -> PRISMA Italy rice) where a **99.49%-train / 97.24%-val 3D-CNN "was not transferable and the results were very poor."** Within-scene Kappa ~0.92-0.97 (Tier-1-adjacent, but these are in-scene pixel splits — cite the *transfer* experiments, not the 99% headline). **Honesty flag: the PRISMA cross-sensor result is proxy-validated (ESA WorldCover + Sentinel-1 rice mask + NDVI + Street View + expert weak labels), NOT field-validated** — the authors call it "feasibility rather than benchmarking accuracy."
- **`kerner2024how` (the Tanzania benchmark for "beat the generic agriculture map").** Design-based accuracy assessment of 11 published cropland maps over **national probability samples in 8 SSA countries including Tanzania** (3,386 samples, Olofsson-style sampling, F1/precision/recall with standard errors). A genuinely stronger evaluation bar than ours, but **binary crop/non-crop** and an intercomparison of *existing maps* (not a trained model). Best maps reach only **F1 ~0.66-0.78** in the better countries; **mean F1 below 0.7 in 7 of 8 countries** (range 0.21 Mali to 0.71 Rwanda); cropland consensus across all 11 maps is **<0.5% of pixels** in every country (Tanzania crop consensus 0.0%).
- **`xun2021novel` (cross-site transfer, but an index/threshold, not a trained classifier — note the nuance).** An unsupervised Cotton Mapping Index (Sentinel-1 VV + Sentinel-2 red-edge `RE1`/`RE2`), developed on US site A and applied **unchanged across sites B-H including US->China** — genuine threshold transfer (Tier 1 in spirit), though because it is rule-based the classic train/test-leakage axis does not apply. Site-A OA/F1 **93.25%/93.27%**; transfer degraded to OA **89.75% / 81.20% / 86.95%** at B/C/D; cross-country Xinjiang area validation R^2 0.74 but MRE 43% at the default threshold (improved to 26.7% after light recalibration). **Binary, single-crop, inside a pre-existing cropland mask** — not multi-class confusion.

### Tier 2 — OUR rung (field-wise grouped CV within one region)

- **`liu2024enhanced` (the deep-learning ceiling AT our exact bar).** A hybrid 3D-CNN + ConvSTAR + 2D-CNN optical+SAR fusion network for Bei'an County, China (2017). Partitioning groups every parcel and a 10 km grid cell into one of train/val/test — explicitly designed to stop same-field pixel leakage, exactly our `field_id`-grouping discipline — **but all data are one county, one year; no spatial/cross-year/cross-sensor transfer.** Same rung as us. Best config (optical + m-chi polarimetric features): **OA 91.7%, Kappa 0.857, mean F1 87.7%** (maize 93.7%, soybean 92.2%, wheat 90.9%, "other crops" F1 collapses to 0.74). Cost: **dual GPUs, 26-step spatio-temporal patches, 21,257 digitized fields.**
- **`mann2025lite` (our study — the anchor).** `StratifiedGroupKFold` (3 splits) grouped on `field_id`, `Field_size`-weighted, LightGBM on 33 `xr_fresh` features, single region / year / sensor. **Kappa 0.82, F1-micro 0.85, balanced accuracy 0.84.** Out-of-field, NOT out-of-region.

### Tier 3 — pooled / random k-fold (leakage; discount heavily)

- **`ogweno2023assessing`** — same-country foil (Kilombero/Ulanga, Morogoro, central/SW Tanzania). **Random 70/30 pixel split, no field grouping, binary cropland vs non-cropland.** Overall accuracies 76-93% (PlanetScope > Sentinel-2) inflated by within-field leakage; not comparable to our Kappa.
- **`zoungrana2024sentinel`** — wheat, Tunisia, Random Forest + SAR-optical fusion in GEE. **Random 70/30 split over pooled sample points, no FID grouping.** Headline OA 95.88% / Kappa 0.90 is a **4-class land-cover** result; the actual wheat-type discrimination is weaker (F1 0.82, Kappa 0.77) and leans on hand-tuned `NDVI`/`MSAVI` thresholds. Quantifies the SAR gain: **~1.5% OA over `S2`-only, ~6% over `S1`-only.**
- **`buttar2024satellite`** (Ayushi & Buttar) — closest East-African smallholder Sentinel-2 DL comparator (CV4A Kenya, 9 classes incl. 4 intercrops). **80/10/10 patch split; field-disjointness not demonstrated (likely field leakage); authors concede cross-site transfer is untested future work.** U-Net **OA 95.3%, F1 73.6%** (the 95.3% is class-imbalance-inflated). Critically, the **RF baseline scored F1 31.35%** — but it was fed *raw bands + NDVI with no engineered temporal features*. Per-class *accuracy*: single crops 64-70%, **intercrops collapse to 15.6-24.9%.**
- **`simon2024exploring`** — Dar es Salaam RF LULC, 7 classes. **Pooled point accuracy, no field-disjoint split**, severe class imbalance (90 agriculture down to 8 water). Kappa 0.77 / 0.86 / 0.77 (1995/2009/2022) — not comparable.
- **`machiwa2021using`** — Msimbazi Basin max-likelihood, 7 classes. **Worse than pooled k-fold:** a single 2016 Sentinel-2 image used as reference for all four epochs (1990-2019) — non-contemporaneous ground truth. Kappa 0.79-0.91 unreliable. "Time-Series" in the title means four decadal snapshots, not within-season sequences.
- **`zhang2008crop`** — N. China maize/cotton, FFT amplitude/phase of MODIS NDVI, max-likelihood + ISODATA. **No confusion matrix, no holdout at all — validation is area-vs-census agreement only** (cotton R^2 0.84, maize R^2 0.71). Below even pooled k-fold; cite for the frequency-domain feature-engineering precedent, not accuracy.
- **`mahlayeye2024characterising`** — maize vs intercropped maize, Busia, Kenya. **Single random 65/35 split of n=87 fields, not cross-validated.** "100% vegetative-phase accuracy" is one split on tens of fields — cite the *phenological-window* finding, not the number.

### Tier 4 — reviews, theory, biophysics, datasets, context (no comparable protocol)

- Reviews on the DL-vs-ML question: `teixeira2023deep` (DL beat ML in **35/36 studies; the lone ML win attributed to small dataset size**), `ma2019deep` (DL's edge largest on curated benchmarks, **lowest and most variable ~91% on medium-resolution LULC — our regime**), `wang2022review` (**DL is data-hungry; current datasets "not sufficient"**), `hossen2025transfer` (the ImageNet-pretrained-CNN foil; cross-dataset collapse 97.4%->63.8%, "satellite TL is in its infancy"), `yadav2024review` (independent survey calling for **lightweight / edge-deployable models**; unaudited tabulated numbers — never cite as benchmarks), `li2023costefficient` (weak-supervision survey — cost-efficient information extraction from massive RS data; cites others' numbers, no original metrics).
- Interpretability: `hohl2024recent` — **SHAP is 38% of RS xAI**, but **SHAP/perturbation methods assume feature independence, "often violated in RS data" by temporal/spectral dependencies** (a direct caveat for our correlated `xr_fresh` features).
- Feature/biophysics lineage: `begue2018remote` (engineered-phenology ancestry of `xr_fresh` — Morton's 36 VI metrics, Fourier/wavelet/DTW; intercropping near-intractable with optical RS), `holzman2021relationship` (**SWIR = liquid water, NIR = dry matter, NDVI/EVI lag** — grounds our `B11`/`B12` water signal; thermal/LST axis absent from Sentinel-2), `fitzgerald2004spider` (interpretable spectral unmixing; **illumination/geometry can swing a feature 5-9x between dates 4 days apart** — motivates temporal aggregation over single-date `hue`/reflectance).
- Policy / standards / data infrastructure: `kerner2024how` (also Tier 1 above) and `begue2020remote` ("generic Africa maps not accurate enough," endorses crowdsourcing + GEE + lightweight ML; names Tanzania via GEOGLAM), `gsars2017handbook` (FAO/GSARS — endorses crowdsourcing, prescribes **design-based probability-sampled accuracy assessment**, a higher bar than our grouped CV), `tseng2021cropharvest` (global benchmark including a **Tanzania rice** set; **the PDF is the GitHub README — no benchmark numbers are extractable, do not cite figures from it**), `osm2024open` (un-bylined, likely LLM-generated web essay — **do not cite directly**).
- Tanzania context: `eilola2021lessons` (**strongest crowdsourcing / YouthMappers anchor** — names YouthMappers, Crowd2Map, HOT, Ramani Huria operating in-country, and documents participatory-data noise/validation difficulty), `reith2021assessment` (croplands are the fastest-expanding and most-degraded class; coarse 300 m maps give 70% degraded vs 16% at 30 m — coarse products miss smallholder change), `simon2024exploring` and `machiwa2021using` (also Tier 3) for the agriculture-encroachment land-change narrative.

## Section 2 — Direct comparators that define our realistic targets (most important section)

Five papers are credibly directly comparable to our study, ordered by how tightly they pin down realistic targets. These set the numbers in Section 8.

1. **`jin2019smallholder` — the calibration anchor (same country, classical ML, bar >= ours).** Tanzania + Kenya smallholder maize, Random Forest in GEE chosen over DL, with honest splits (field-disjoint maize; district-disjoint cropland; independent crop-cut yield). Its candid, leakage-controlled **maize accuracy of 79% in Tanzania (63% Kenya)** is the appropriate yardstick. Against it, our **Kappa 0.82 / F1-micro 0.85 over a much harder seven-crop multi-class problem (optical-only)** looks strong — but the comparison must foreground that their 79% is a *binary* maize/non-maize number. Their Tanzania feature ranking puts **SWIR (`B11`/`B12`-derived `STI`/`NDTI`) and red-edge indices at the top**, independently validating our `B11`, `B12`, `B6` choices. Their in-house GEE point-labeler is a direct precedent for our YouthMappers crowdsourcing.
2. **`liu2024enhanced` — the deep-learning ceiling at our exact bar.** Field+grid-grouped CV within one county-year (same rung as us). Best **Kappa 0.857, mean F1 87.7%** — only marginally above our **Kappa 0.82 / F1-micro 0.85**, achieved with **dual GPUs, 26-step patches, and 21,257 digitized fields** versus our GPU-free, crowdsourced-label, optical-only pipeline. Their own ablation shows the **hand-engineered m-chi polarimetric features (not architectural depth) drove the largest gains** — the single most direct external support that "features > depth." Their minority "other crops" F1 of 0.74 calibrates how hard residual minority classes remain even for a tuned deep net.
3. **`buttar2024satellite` (Ayushi & Buttar) — the closest like-for-like East-African smallholder DL foil (Tier 3, discount).** CV4A Kenya, Sentinel-2 10 m, overlapping crops (maize, cassava), intercrops. Its **RF-at-F1-31.35%** is the headline cautionary tale — but it failed because it was fed *raw bands + NDVI with no engineered temporal features*. We frame our result as a **feature-engineering** win, not a model-class win: same tree family, but `xr_fresh` statistics first. Its **intercrop collapse (15.6-24.9% accuracy)** quantifies the structural ceiling our minority crops push against. Numbers discounted for unproven field-disjointness; the patch-split 95.3% OA is not comparable to our grouped-CV Kappa.
4. **`kerner2024how` — the Tanzania benchmark for the "beat the generic agriculture map" claim (Tier 1, but binary and existing-maps).** Tanzania-specific cropland F1 for 11 published maps, best **~0.66-0.78**, mean below 0.7. Our crop-type map should be positioned as outperforming these generic products in-region — while being explicit that their probability-sampled F1 estimates *population-level map accuracy* on an *easier binary* task, not our within-sample multi-class generalization.
5. **`pankajakshan2025deep` — the transfer foil above our bar.** Best external evidence for the direction "lite > heavy under shift" (their 99% 3D-CNN fails to transfer; their 2-parameter classical pipeline succeeds), and their named fix for residual spectral confusion is exactly our lever — *multi-temporal analysis*. But it clears a **higher** evaluation bar (true cross-scene/cross-sensor, even if PRISMA is proxy-validated), so we cite it for the *argument*, never for parity of rigor.

Honorable mentions for design corroboration (not headline targets): `zoungrana2024sentinel` (classical-ML cousin; SAR gain only ~1.5% over good optical) and `mahlayeye2024characterising` (phenological-window dependence + intercropping confusion).

## Section 3 — Feature-design findings (discounted by tier)

**Engineered time-series features over raw sequences — well supported, mostly Tier 4 lineage plus Tier 1/2 evidence.**

- The "compress a phenological sequence into engineered descriptors, then classify" recipe is a mature, long-established branch: `begue2018remote` (Tier 4) documents the ancestry — Morton's **36 VI metrics** (min/max/mean of NDVI/EVI), Fourier/wavelet transforms, DTW shape matching, bag-of-temporal-SIFT — direct relatives of our `xr_fresh` mean/max/slope/skewness/number-of-peaks/complexity. `zhang2008crop` (Tier 3) is an early frequency-domain instance (FFT amplitude/phase of NDVI). `reith2021assessment` (Tier 4) uses the same trend/state/performance NDVI decomposition.
- **Features beat depth — the strongest single finding, from a deep-learning paper's own ablation.** `liu2024enhanced` (Tier 2): switching from raw backscatter to hand-engineered **m-chi polarimetric features lifted OA by >20 points and Kappa by >30 points** for the 1D model; the input representation mattered more than the architecture differences among the deep nets. `buttar2024satellite` (Tier 3): RF collapsed to F1 31.35% *without* engineered temporal features — strong (if leaky-protocol) evidence that the feature engineering, not the GPU, is what makes classical ML competitive.
- **Temporal features matter for cropland itself.** `kerner2024how` (Tier 1): the only two maps with no temporal input (Esri, Dynamic World) underperformed; the authors attribute better maps' edge partly to temporal information.

**Band/index choices (`EVI`, `B2`, `B6` red-edge, `B11`/`B12` SWIR, `hue`) — well corroborated.**

- **SWIR (`B11`/`B12`).** `jin2019smallholder` (Tier 1) ranks SWIR-derived `STI`/`NDTI` and `SWIR1`/`SWIR2` bands **first, second, fifth, sixth, ninth** in the Tanzania feature importance — the strongest in-country validation. `holzman2021relationship` (Tier 4) gives the biophysical reason: **SWIR tracks liquid water directly and early, NIR tracks dry matter, and NDVI/EVI lag** — so SWIR features are not redundant with greenness and plausibly separate water-strategy-distinct crops (rice standing water; sorghum/millet drought tolerance; cotton's long water demand). `zoungrana2024sentinel` (Tier 3) reaches Kappa 0.86 on `S2`-only with `B11`/`B12` retained at resampling cost. `liu2024enhanced` (Tier 2) selected `B11` SWIR for soybean/maize separability.
- **Red-edge (`B6`).** `xun2021novel` (Tier 1) shows red-edge `RE1`/`RE2` carry cotton-discriminating information (one of our hard minority crops). `mahlayeye2024characterising` (Tier 3) finds red-edge and NIR the most valuable regions for maize/intercrop separation. `liu2024enhanced` (Tier 2) and `jin2019smallholder` (Tier 1) both rank red-edge highly.
- **`hue` and reflectance robustness.** `fitzgerald2004spider` (Tier 4) is a concrete warning that geometry-sensitive features (like our HSV `hue` and raw reflectance) can swing 5-9x between near-adjacent dates from sun angle/shade alone — a direct argument that our **multi-temporal statistics are more robust than any single-date `hue` value**, since aggregating across the season averages out illumination noise.

**Silences / what features cannot reach (optical-only design boundary).**

- The **thermal/LST axis that `holzman2021relationship` shows adds the most to water-availability estimation is absent from Sentinel-2** — irrigated-vs-rainfed and fine water-stress timing signals are structurally out of reach without Landsat/MODIS LST or ECOSTRESS fusion.
- **SAR fusion is the lever we forgo.** `zoungrana2024sentinel` (Tier 3, ~1.5% OA over good optical), `liu2024enhanced` (Tier 2, m-chi the dominant gain), `xun2021novel` (Tier 1, VV improves cotton), `jin2019smallholder` (Tier 1, `S1` "particularly useful" in cloudy Tanzania), and `teixeira2023deep` (Tier 4, fusion repeatedly improves accuracy) all show SAR helps — most in cloudy conditions like our Masika season. The honest read: the optical->fusion gain is real but often modest (~1.5% in good optical) and smaller than the optimism baked into the leaky protocols of the papers reporting it.

## Section 4 — Model-architecture findings (classical ML vs CNN/RNN/U-Net; discounted by tier)

**Classical ML is competitive specifically in the data-scarce / shifting regime — the thesis, with mixed-tier support.**

- **The cleanest quantitative anchor:** `teixeira2023deep` (Tier 4 review) — **DL beat ML in 35 of 36 studies, and the single ML win was explicitly attributed to small dataset size** (an SVM on Sentinel-2 NDVI time series beating CNN/RNN under stratified 10-fold CV — a near-miniature of our design). This lets us claim "we operate in the regime where ML competes," not "ML is generally better."
- **DL's edge is regime-dependent and weakest in our regime:** `ma2019deep` (Tier 4) — DL's median OA is highest on curated benchmarks (~95% scene), **lowest and most variable on medium-resolution LULC (~91%) — exactly Sentinel-2-scale crop mapping.** `wang2022review` and `hossen2025transfer` (Tier 4) document DL's data hunger and that "satellite TL is in its infancy."
- **Heavy nets fail to transfer where lite models succeed:** `pankajakshan2025deep` (Tier 1) — a 99% 3D-CNN "was not transferable, results were very poor," while a 2-parameter classical pipeline transferred cross-scene/cross-sensor. `hossen2025transfer` (Tier 4) — CNN cross-dataset collapse **97.4% -> 63.8%**.
- **Even a winning deep net barely exceeds us, at far higher cost:** `liu2024enhanced` (Tier 2), Kappa 0.857 vs our 0.82, with dual GPUs and 21,257 fields.
- **Tree-based ML is a recognized strong baseline, not a fallback:** `jin2019smallholder` (Tier 1, RF at national scale, chosen for portability), `zoungrana2024sentinel` and `simon2024exploring` (Tier 3, RF on Tanzanian LULC), `yadav2024review` (Tier 4, "RF demonstrated high accuracy compared to other models"), `tseng2021cropharvest` (Tier 4, RF is the advertised benchmark baseline).

**Where DL/U-Net genuinely wins (honest scoping).**

- `buttar2024satellite` (Tier 3): all deep segmentation models beat classical ML on the CV4A patch split — but the RF baseline lacked engineered features, and the split's field-disjointness is unproven. `teixeira2023deep` (Tier 4): DL dominates when data are abundant. The honest framing is *regime*, not universal superiority.

**Class-count and non-crop classes.**

- `teixeira2023deep` (Tier 4): including non-crop classes improves overall accuracy — supports our inclusion of urban/forest/shrub/tidal/water. Performance degrades when classes share phenology — relevant to our cereal confusions (maize/sorghum/millet).

## Section 5 — Conceptual / theoretical framing

- **Inductive bias and transfer.** The lite-learning case rests on inductive bias matched to the problem: engineered phenological features encode the temporal structure deep nets must learn from scarce data. `pankajakshan2025deep` (Tier 1) embodies this (a 2-parameter spatial-spectral prior transfers where a high-capacity 3D-CNN overfits); `hossen2025transfer` (Tier 4) catalogues negative transfer (ImageNet features "not tailored to agriculture, potentially leading to negative transfer"). Our `xr_fresh` features are purpose-built for phenology and need no pretraining corpus.
- **Interpretability / SHAP.** `hohl2024recent` (Tier 4) backs the *choice* (SHAP = 38% of RS xAI, the community-standard model-agnostic interpreter) but raises the load-bearing caveat: **SHAP assumes feature independence, "often violated in RS data due to scale, geographical relationships, and temporal dependencies."** Our `xr_fresh` features are strongly correlated (many statistics per band, correlated bands), so this caveat applies squarely — SHAP can split or mis-attribute credit among correlated features. Our classical-on-named-features setup partly sidesteps deep-net xAI pain points (no patch saliency), which is a qualitative advantage worth claiming, but the correlation caveat must be stated.
- **Deep-learning data hunger and compute cost.** `wang2022review`, `ma2019deep`, `hossen2025transfer`, `teixeira2023deep` (all Tier 4) converge: high-performing DL needs large, well-annotated datasets and GPU infrastructure that data-scarce sub-Saharan smallholder settings lack. `yadav2024review` (Tier 4) independently calls for lightweight/edge models. `mann2025lite` itself frames CPU-only, 10-15-minute tuning as the efficiency payoff.
- **Optical vs SAR.** SAR adds the most where optical is cloud-degraded (`jin2019smallholder` Tier 1; `liu2024enhanced` Tier 2; `xun2021novel` Tier 1), but the marginal gain over good optical is modest (`zoungrana2024sentinel` Tier 3, ~1.5%). The trade is cheapness/simplicity vs cloud robustness.
- **Crowdsourced-label noise.** `gsars2017handbook` (Tier 4) endorses crowdsourcing in principle but flags unresolved representativeness/bias; `eilola2021lessons` (Tier 4) documents in-country YouthMappers/HOT/Crowd2Map/Ramani Huria infrastructure *and* the "trust the image, skip ground verification" / "hard to validate participatory data" risks; `begue2020remote` (Tier 4) endorses crowdsourcing as part of the solution to SSA's binding ground-data constraint. Our field-size buffering, multi-source label merging, and field-disjoint grouping are principled responses to participatory-label noise.
- **Design-based vs model-based evaluation.** `gsars2017handbook` and `kerner2024how` (Tier 1/4) prescribe/use design-based, probability-sampled accuracy assessment — a *higher* bar than our grouped CV, which measures model generalization within the labelled sample, not population-level map accuracy. This bounds what our reported numbers mean.

## Section 6 — Project-specific caveats surfaced by the audit

1. **Our bar is field-grouped CV within one region, not a spatial holdout.** Our Kappa 0.82 / F1-micro 0.85 are an **optimistic upper bound on cross-region or cross-year transfer.** `jin2019smallholder` (Tier 1) demonstrates the gap is real: its Kenya/Tanzania selected feature sets were almost entirely different (only Longitude common in the cropland top-10), so even within East Africa a single-region model does not trivially transfer. `pankajakshan2025deep` and `hossen2025transfer` show heavy models lose 30+ points under shift; lite models are not immune. We must not present our results as equivalently rigorous to the Tier-1 comparators.
2. **Single year (2023), optical-only Sentinel-2 (months 1-8).** No cross-year robustness check (`jin2019smallholder` quietly warns accuracy *dropped* when more cross-year data were added); no SAR (the modest-but-real fusion gain is forgone); no thermal/LST (water-availability signal `holzman2021relationship` shows is structurally unreachable).
3. **Drought-confounded single collection window (April-May 2023, late season).** `mahlayeye2024characterising` (Tier 3) shows crop separability is **phase-gated** (present only in vegetative and flowering-yield windows, absent at ripening/post-harvest) — our late-season capture may sit at the low-separability end for some crops, a caution our season-spanning statistics partially but not fully mitigate.
4. **Crowdsourced YouthMappers labels carry geolocation/label noise.** Documented in-country and in-principle by `eilola2021lessons` and `gsars2017handbook` (Tier 4). Photo-based verification, no independent post-classification field check.
5. **Intercropping is a known, partly irreducible optical-RS confusion source for our minority crops.** `begue2018remote` (Tier 4): discriminating intercrops "based only on their spectral signature is almost impossible" at medium resolution. `mahlayeye2024characterising` (Tier 3): intercropped maize is spectrally near-indistinguishable from monocrop at most phases. `buttar2024satellite` (Tier 3): intercrop classes collapse to 15.6-24.9%. This frames our cassava/maize, sorghum, millet, cotton difficulty as a structural ceiling, not just a modeling shortfall — motivating `Field_size`-aware sampling and, in future, higher resolution.
6. **Minority-crop recall is the load-bearing result, not the 0.85 F1-micro.** The aggregate is buoyed by maize/rice and easy non-crop classes. Honest comparison with other papers must track per-class minority recall.
7. **SHAP feature-independence violation** (`hohl2024recent`, Tier 4) means our SHAP rankings over correlated `xr_fresh` features should be presented as suggestive-and-cross-fold-consistent, not as a validated causal account.

## Section 8 — Bottom-line numerical targets the literature supports

All targets below are stated **under our field-grouped-CV (Tier-2) bar**. None is validated under true spatial or cross-year transfer; the literature is explicit that performance falls under genuine transfer, and we have no in-corpus number that licenses a transfer target for a multi-class smallholder crop model.

**Headline aggregate (achieved, and defensible as a target at our bar):**
- **Cohen's Kappa ~0.82-0.86** is the realistic ceiling at our bar. `liu2024enhanced` (Tier 2, same rung) reaches **Kappa 0.857 / mean F1 87.7%** with a heavy GPU pipeline; our **0.82 / F1-micro 0.85** sits just below that and is therefore a strong, honest result. Treat **Kappa >= 0.80** as the success threshold and **~0.86** as the practical ceiling at this protocol; do not chase higher without suspecting leakage.
- **F1-micro ~0.85** is consistent with the Tier-2 ceiling but is *buoyed by majority and non-crop classes* — it is not the load-bearing number.

**Macro / balanced metrics (the honest aggregate):**
- Target **macro-F1 in the ~0.75-0.85 band** at our bar. Justification: `liu2024enhanced`'s mean F1 of 87.7% includes a minority class at 0.74, so a macro average pulled below the micro figure is expected and healthy. Report macro-F1 explicitly; a macro-F1 noticeably below micro-F1 is the *correct* signature of minority-crop difficulty, not a failure.

**Per-class targets:**
- **Easy classes (rice, maize, water, tidal):** F1 / accuracy **>= 0.82-0.92** is achievable and expected (our rice 92%, maize 82%; `liu2024enhanced` maize 93.7%, soybean 92.2%). Target **>= 0.85**.
- **Hard minority crops (cassava, millet, sunflower, sorghum, cotton):** realistic, literature-anchored target is **per-class F1 ~0.50-0.75**, with the lower end honest. Anchors: `liu2024enhanced`'s best-model minority "other crops" F1 = **0.74** (a tuned deep net's floor at our rung); `buttar2024satellite`'s intercrop classes **0.16-0.25** (the structural floor when intercropping dominates and no engineered features are used); `begue2018remote`/`mahlayeye2024characterising` frame intercropped-crop separability as near the optical-RS limit. **Do not promise minority F1 > 0.75 under our protocol**; treat **>= 0.55 minority recall** as a meaningful success and **~0.74** as the aspirational ceiling.

**"Beat the generic map" target (different, easier task — for the cropland-detection framing only):**
- Our crop-type map, when collapsed to crop/non-crop for comparison, should exceed `kerner2024how`'s Tanzania cropland ceiling of **F1 ~0.66-0.78** (mean across maps < 0.7). This is a Tier-1, binary, probability-sampled number — cite it as the bar to beat, not as a like-for-like multi-class comparison.

**How much lower under genuine transfer (explicit, literature-bounded):**
- Expect a **substantial drop under cross-region or cross-year transfer** that we have NOT measured. The corpus quantifies analogous drops: `xun2021novel` cross-site OA degradation of **~4-12 points** (93% -> 81-90%); `hossen2025transfer` cross-dataset **97.4% -> 63.8%** (~34 points); `jin2019smallholder`'s near-total feature-set divergence between Kenya and Tanzania; and `jin2019smallholder`'s candid cross-year warning that accuracy *fell* when more cross-year data were added. A defensible expectation: **our Kappa could fall by 0.10-0.25+ under a true held-out-region or held-out-year test.** Until we run the Section-7 spatial holdout, every target above is an **in-region upper bound**, and the manuscript must say so.
