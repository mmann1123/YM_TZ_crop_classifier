# Lite Learning: Efficient Crop Classification in Tanzania Using Feature Extraction with Machine Learning & Crowd Sourcing

**Citation:** Mann, M. L., Colson, L., Nealon, R., Engstrom, R., & Nakacwa, S. (under review, Revision 2, PDF generated 23 Jan 2026). *Lite Learning: Efficient Crop Classification in Tanzania Using Feature Extraction with Machine Learning & Crowd Sourcing*. Submitted to IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing (JSTARS). Manuscript ID `JSTARS-2025-00807`; submission `6fef58e7-c54c-424e-94aa-537e364dc008`. DOI: not found in PDF (manuscript under review; no DOI assigned).

> Note: This briefer documents the manuscript itself — the anchor against which every other paper in this corpus is calibrated, not an external comparator. The "our study" lens used in the sibling briefers is defined here.

## Objectives

Propose a "lite learning" alternative to deep learning for smallholder crop-type classification in data-scarce sub-Saharan settings. Two stated challenges: (1) the lack of in-season, multi-class crop-type training data in developing regions, and (2) obtaining high-accuracy crop-type predictions from limited training data without the labeled-data volume, GPU compute, and interpretability cost of deep learning. The approach pairs crowdsourced ground truth (YouthMappers) with automated time-series feature extraction from Sentinel-2, fed to classical tree-based machine learning, with SHAP for interpretation. Target crops: maize (Masika), rice, sorghum, millet, sunflower, cassava, cotton (the last five are the hard minority crops); plus non-crop classes water, tidal, forest, shrub, urban.

## Methods

- **Imagery and bands.** Sentinel-2 L2 harmonized surface reflectance, monthly composites January–August 2023, 10 m. Cloud/shadow masking via `s2cloudless` (cloud-probability threshold 50%) on Google Earth Engine; linear interpolation fills cloud gaps. Bands/index used: `B2` (blue), `B6` (red edge), `B8` (NIR), `B11`/`B12` (SWIR), plus `EVI` and `hue`.
- **Engineered time-series features (the core method).** The custom `xr_fresh` toolkit computes tsfresh-style per-pixel statistics on `B12`, `B11`, `hue`, `B6`, `EVI`, `B2`: absolute energy, absolute sum of changes, mean absolute change, mean change, autocorrelation (lags 1–3), count above/below mean, day-of-year of max/min, kurtosis, skewness, quantiles (q0.05, q0.95), standard deviation, variance, linear time trend, longest strike above/below mean, mean second derivative, ratio beyond r-sigma, time-series complexity, and symmetry. This replaces raw temporal sequences fed to RNN/CNN/patch networks.
- **Ground truth.** Crowdsourced via YouthMappers (KoboCollect/KoboToolbox), late April–May 2023, mid-season phenology. 1,720 raw observations cleaned to 1,400+ crop entries plus 386 non-crop observations; photo-based remote verification removed ~320. Fields ≥30 m across; ≥1 km separation between sites to reduce spatial autocorrelation. Additional non-agricultural training from high-res Google Earth imagery.
- **Field-size buffering.** Pixels extracted with a buffer around each field point (Small 5 m, Medium 10 m, Large 30 m) so multiple adjacent pixels per field are captured; larger buffers hurt performance because of within-field heterogeneity.
- **Model selection.** Optuna (100 trials) over LightGBM, SVC, RandomForest; variance-threshold filter (variance < 0.5 dropped); standard scaling. LightGBM selected.
- **Feature selection.** SHAP computed per fold; union of top-30 mean-|SHAP| and top-30 max-|SHAP| features → 33 final features from hundreds of candidates.
- **Evaluation protocol (load-bearing).** `StratifiedGroupKFold` with **3 splits, grouped on `field_id`**, so all pixels from one field stay together in either train or validation — preventing the within-field pixel leakage that inflates accuracy. Primary metric Cohen's Kappa (chosen for class imbalance); also reports balanced accuracy, F1-micro, precision/recall. This places the paper at the **field-wise (FID) grouped k-fold within one region** rung of the generalization spectrum. **It does NOT use a spatially disjoint holdout (a different tile, region, or agro-ecological zone), nor cross-year or cross-sensor transfer.** All data come from three northern districts (Arusha, Dodoma, Mwanza), single year (2023), single sensor (Sentinel-2). The "out-of-sample" results are out-of-field, not out-of-region.
- **Computational efficiency claim.** LightGBM training is histogram-based, `O(n·m) + O(b·k·d)`; full 100-trial / 3-fold tuning runs in 10–15 min on a CPU-only laptop (Intel i7, 16GB RAM). The paper contrasts this with LSTM (`O(e·n·t·(h²+h·i))`) and 2D/3D U-Net cost, and frames CPU-only inference as deployable in resource-constrained settings and lower-carbon.

## Key Findings

- **Headline:** Cohen's Kappa 0.82, F1-micro 0.85, balanced accuracy 0.84, precision 0.86, recall 0.84 — out-of-sample under the field-grouped CV scheme, across seven crop types and six land-cover classes.
- **Per-class (the load-bearing result, not the F1-micro):** rice 92% and maize 82% out-of-sample accuracy; millet, sunflower, tidal, water, shrub, forest each >73%. **Sorghum, cassava, and cotton show moderate confusion** — cassava confused with maize (likely intercropping at 10 m), forest confused with shrub. Minority-crop recall is what the "lite learning" thesis actually rests on; the aggregate F1-micro is buoyed by easy majority and non-crop classes.
- **Beats the generic "agriculture" map:** matched or exceeded broadly-used global/regional land-cover products for Tanzania (compared against Kerner et al. 2024), despite performing the harder per-crop task while those products emit a single undifferentiated "agriculture" class.
- **Interpretability via SHAP:** `B11.mean` (mean SWIR) separates dryland-adapted crops (sunflower, sorghum, millet) by persistent canopy-water differences; `hue.quantile.q.0.05` distinguishes rice/sunflower; `B12.absolute.sum.of.changes` separates cotton/shrub/forest by temporal volatility of cotton's managed phenology (citing Xun et al. 2021); `EVI.standard.deviation` isolates urban (low greenness variation).

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This *is* our study. It defines the evaluation bar — **field-wise grouped CV within one region** — against which the rest of the corpus is judged. When other briefers flag a comparator as "clearing a higher bar," they mean it performs true spatial, cross-year, or cross-sensor transfer that this manuscript does not attempt. The engineered-feature-vs-deep-learning trade-off, the SHAP-driven interpretability, the SWIR/red-edge/hue band rationale, the smallholder data-scarcity framing, and the field-grouping anti-leakage discipline are all set here.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **In-region-only bar.** The cardinal limitation: evaluation is field-disjoint but **not** region-disjoint. The model is trained and tested entirely within Arusha, Dodoma, and Mwanza. Geographic transferability to other agro-ecological zones, farming practices, or crop varieties is explicitly stated as untested. The "out-of-sample" language is correct (out-of-field) but must not be read as spatial transfer.
- **Single year, single sensor.** All data are 2023 Sentinel-2 optical. No cross-year robustness check; no SAR fusion (flagged as future work to handle persistent cloud). Cross-sensor transfer is absent.
- **Minority-crop recall is the load-bearing result, not the 0.85 F1-micro.** The aggregate metrics are inflated by maize, rice, and non-crop classes. Sorghum, cassava, and cotton remain weak. Honest comparison with other papers should track per-class minority-crop recall, not the headline.
- **Drought-confounded single collection window.** The April–May 2023 collection coincided with drought, producing fields at varying phenological stages and early harvests; crops were captured primarily in late season. Monthly composites may miss double-cropping and short-duration stages; linear interpolation assumes gradual transitions.
- **Class imbalance handled by exclusion.** Under-represented crops (peanuts, soybeans, okra) were dropped rather than modeled, which removes them from both the difficulty and the headline.
- **Photo-based label verification, no post-classification field check.** Labels were remotely verified from field photos; independent ground-truthing of model predictions was not performed — a stated methodological limitation.
- **Feature-count interpretability tension.** 33 features still challenge intuitive interpretation despite SHAP.
