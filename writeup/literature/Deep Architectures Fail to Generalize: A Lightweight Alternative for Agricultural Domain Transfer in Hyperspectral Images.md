# Deep Architectures Fail to Generalize: A Lightweight Alternative for Agricultural Domain Transfer in Hyperspectral Images

**Citation:** Pankajakshan, P., Padmasanan, A., & Sundar, S. (2025). "Deep Architectures Fail to Generalize: A Lightweight Alternative for Agricultural Domain Transfer in Hyperspectral Images." *Sensors*, 26(1), 174. DOI: `10.3390/s26010174` (verified via Crossref; title, authors, journal match exactly).

> **STRONGEST ON-POINT COMPARATOR in the corpus — but it clears a HIGHER bar than ours.** This is a true cross-scene / cross-sensor domain-transfer study (train on one benchmark scene, infer on a different scene and on a different sensor with no retraining). Our manuscript's evaluation sits at field-wise grouped-CV-within-one-region; this paper does spatial + cross-domain transfer. Flag for honest scoping: it is harder evidence than ours, and we should not claim parity of evaluation rigor with it. Its headline finding — that a tiny GPU-free classical model transfers across domains where a high-accuracy 3D-CNN collapses — is nonetheless the single best external support for our "lite learning beats heavy deep nets in data-scarce / shifting settings" thesis.

## Objectives

Build a lightweight, low-resource hyperspectral image (HSI) classifier that explicitly balances **spatial nearness** and **spectral similarity**, trained on closed-set benchmark scenes but designed to **generalize to open-set agricultural scenarios** (class-distribution shift, novel and absent classes) with only 1-10% labeled data and at most two tunable parameters. Concretely, demonstrate domain generalization to unseen crops, seasons, and regions without retraining, with paddy/flooded-rice extent mapping in Italy as the real-world target.

## Methods

- **Sensors / data:** Three open AVIRIS/ROSIS HSI benchmarks for training/testing — Indian Pines, Salinas, Pavia University (closed-set, 103-224 bands). For transfer: AVIRIS Kennedy Space Center and Hyperion Botswana scenes, and — the real-world test — an **ASI PRISMA** hyperspectral scene over the Piedmont/Novara rice region of Italy (different sensor, 30 m, 171 SWIR bands).
- **Model (the "lite" alternative):** PCA to a low-dimensional spectral manifold (>=90% explained variance), then a pixel-wise **SVM (RBF)** spectral classifier (GPU-capable via ThunderSVM but not GPU-required) producing an initial class-probability map, followed by an **iterative unsupervised refinement** that trades off two Gaussian kernels — a spatial-nearness kernel `S_i` and a spectral-similarity kernel `W_i` — in a CRF/ICM-like energy minimization, plus ensemble majority voting and weak labeling. **At most two dataset-specific parameters** (`C`, `gamma`) need tuning; four others (`r`, `sigma_w`, `sigma_s`, `beta`) are fixed and transferable across domains. Compared against EPF, IFRF, RMG and against transformer SOTA (MASSFormer, SpectralFormer).
- **Feature/granularity design:** Pixel-wise spectral classification regularized by a local spatial neighborhood (radius `r`, optimal `r=4`); not patch-CNN, not raw-sequence-RNN. Conceptually aligned with our preference for cheap, interpretable features + a light learner over heavy end-to-end nets.
- **Evaluation protocol (load-bearing — the reason this paper matters):**
  - *Closed-set benchmarks:* training fractions deliberately reduced **below** conventional splits to simulate label scarcity (Indian Pines 10%, Salinas 1% vs literature 4%, Pavia 1% vs literature 6%); parameters via 5-fold CV grid search; metrics OA, AA (average/macro accuracy), and **Cohen's Kappa**. Reported per-class accuracy tables (Tables 3-5). On these *within-scene* splits the proposed method reaches OA ~97-99% / Kappa ~0.92-0.97, comparable to or matching transformer SOTA at far lower label and compute budgets.
  - *Cross-scene transfer (stronger than our bar):* parameters fixed at the transferable set, model **inferred on Botswana and KSC scenes it was not trained on**, with no retraining — Botswana OA/Kappa 90.1%/93.9% (spectral) rising to 97.4%/98.3% (spectral-spatial); KSC 85.2%->97.1% OA. This is genuine cross-scene generalization.
  - *Cross-sensor, cross-region transfer (the headline):* a model **trained on AVIRIS Indian Pines (Indiana, USA) is inferred on the PRISMA Piedmont (Italy) rice scene** — different sensor, different continent, mostly non-overlapping classes (paddy, an unseen crop). This is the top rung of our spectrum (cross-domain/cross-sensor), well above field-grouped CV.
  - *Critical honesty caveat (their own):* **the PRISMA transfer has NO field ground truth.** Validation is **proxy / multi-sensor cross-validation** — agreement with ESA WorldCover, a Sentinel-1 VH flooded-rice mask, Sentinel-2 NDVI signatures, CORINE, and Google Street View, with weak labels assigned by subject-matter experts. The authors explicitly state "the absence of field campaigns for PRISMA means the methodology depends on proxy validation rather than actual field campaign data" and call the PRISMA result "feasibility rather than benchmarking accuracy." So the cross-sensor rung is demonstrated but **proxy-validated, not field-validated**.

## Key Findings

- **The load-bearing comparator result:** the authors trained a deep **3D-CNN** (spectral+spatial) that reached **99.49% training accuracy and 97.24% validation accuracy on Indian Pines** (60/20/20 split), then inferred it on the Barengo/Piedmont study area — and **"the model was not transferable and the results were very poor."** Their two-parameter SVM-based method transferred where the 3D-CNN collapsed. This is a clean, citable instance of "high in-distribution accuracy, catastrophic out-of-domain failure" for deep nets, with a classical alternative succeeding.
- Across benchmarks the lite method improves OA by 7.22-15% over spectral-only baselines and matches/exceeds transformer SOTA (e.g. matching MASSFormer-class accuracy) while needing only 1-10% labels and 2 tunable parameters; training+inference < 21 s on CPU vs minutes-to-server-GPU for the deep models.
- **Spectral confusion is the residual error mode**, and their stated fix is multi-temporal data: "the model confuses spectrally similar crops or the same crop under different management... typical of single temporal snapshot-based analysis. **Multi-temporal analysis might resolve this confusion.**" Greatest confusion was Soybean-mintill vs Corn-notill — same-season residue/tillage ambiguity from a single date.
- Ablation: pixel-wise SVM alone is noisy/fragmented; +CRF spatial regularization helps dominant classes but can hurt small/ambiguous ones; the full weak-label + iterative-refinement pipeline is most balanced (OA ~93%).
- Honest scoping by the authors: the lite framework "is not a replacement for deep networks where data are abundant, diverse, or well-annotated"; transformers win in closed-set, >10%-label settings.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

- **Best external evidence for the "lite learning" thesis.** A 2-parameter, GPU-free classical pipeline transfers across scenes and sensors while a 99%-accurate 3D-CNN fails to generalize — almost exactly the "cheap, interpretable, GPU-free model matches/beats heavy deep nets in data-scarce, shifting settings" argument our paper makes. Cite the 3D-CNN-collapse-vs-SVM-transfer contrast directly.
- **It validates our central lever from the other side.** Their named fix for residual spectral confusion is *multi-temporal analysis* — which is precisely what our `xr_fresh` engineered time-series features deliver. They identify the gap (single-snapshot spectral confusion) that our temporal-feature approach is designed to close. Strong complementary framing: their spatial-spectral lite model + our multi-temporal lite features address the same problem from orthogonal angles.
- **Honest-scoping obligation.** Because this paper clears a higher evaluation bar (true cross-domain transfer, even if proxy-validated) than our field-grouped-CV-within-northern-Tanzania, we must NOT present our results as equivalently rigorous on transfer. Use it to argue the *direction* (lite > heavy under shift) while explicitly noting their evaluation is harder and ours is in-region grouped CV.
- **Sensor/feature contrast.** Hyperspectral (100s of bands) + spatial-nearness regularization, single-date; ours is multispectral Sentinel-2 + multi-temporal engineered features. Different data, convergent conclusion that classical/light methods are the right tool in low-label agriculture.
- **Minority-crop relevance.** Their open-set framing (novel/absent crop classes, e.g. paddy unseen in training) speaks to our hard minority crops; their finding that the iterative refinement handles under-represented/unseen classes better than the deep net is encouraging for our cassava/millet/sunflower/sorghum problem.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **Clears a HIGHER bar than ours — calibrate accordingly.** Cross-scene (Botswana, KSC) and cross-sensor/cross-region (Indian Pines -> PRISMA Italy) transfer is genuinely above our field-wise grouped-CV-within-one-region rung. Do not imply our evaluation is as stringent.
- **PRISMA cross-sensor transfer is proxy-validated, NOT field-validated.** No field campaign for the Italy scene; "ground truth" is ESA WorldCover + Sentinel-1 rice mask + NDVI signatures + Street View + expert weak labels. The authors themselves frame it as feasibility, not measured accuracy. So the strongest (cross-sensor) rung lacks independent field labels — temper any claim that this is a fully validated transfer result.
- **The within-benchmark accuracies are pixel-level on classic HSI scenes.** OA/AA/Kappa on Indian Pines/Salinas/Pavia come from train/test fractions of pixels within a single scene (closed-set), which is the same in-scene regime our manuscript distinguishes from spatial transfer — the *transfer* experiments, not these, are the higher-bar evidence. Cite the protocol, not the 99.42% Salinas headline, when comparing.
- **Class-imbalance reporting is good:** AA (macro accuracy) and Cohen's Kappa reported alongside OA, and per-class accuracy tables given — so minority-class performance is visible, not hidden. (Caveat: some tiny classes, e.g. Stone-steel-towers, Building-grass-trees, show much lower per-class accuracy than the OA headline, the usual imbalance artifact.)
- **No field-grouped CV / FID-disjoint splitting is reported for the benchmark scenes** — within-scene pixel splits risk the usual spatial-autocorrelation inflation; the paper's defense against this is the separate cross-scene transfer experiments rather than grouped CV.
- **Phenology sensitivity (their stated limitation):** accuracy is best at crop maturity; single-date spectral snapshots underperform off-peak — again pointing to the value of the multi-temporal features our pipeline uses.
