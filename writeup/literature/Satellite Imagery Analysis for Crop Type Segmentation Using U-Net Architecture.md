> **This is the strongest direct comparator in this batch:** a Global-South, Sentinel-2 10 m, smallholder, multi-temporal deep-learning crop study on the CV4A Kenya dataset, with explicit maize/cassava + intercrop classes that mirror our minority-crop difficulty.

## Objectives

- Generate per-pixel crop-type maps for the difficult smallholder-farm setting (small fields, sparse labels, persistent cloud) by recasting crop-type *classification* as semantic *segmentation*.
- Apply a U-Net encoder-decoder to a multi-temporal Sentinel-2 time series and benchmark it against classical ML (Random Forest, CatBoost, KNN) and other deep segmentation networks (FCN, SegNet).
- Demonstrate the approach on an openly available developing-nation dataset (CV4A Kenya), chosen precisely because smallholder African fields are smaller and label-poorer than US-style benchmarks.

## Methods

- **Dataset:** Radiant Earth **CV4A Kenya crop-type detection** dataset — Sentinel-2 (10 m, the abstract notes an 8-band input stack) multi-temporal scenes over Kenyan smallholder farms during a single (unusual) growing season. **9 crop classes: 5 single crops + 4 intercropped classes** (maize/common bean, cassava/common bean, maize/soybean, and maize/cassava). This intercrop structure is the defining feature for our purposes.
- **Preprocessing (eo-learn):** cloud removal via the dataset's cloud-probability layer binarized with Otsu thresholding (drop scenes >70% cloud); `NDVI` computed from `NIR`/`Red`; temporal interpolation to fill missing/gappy scenes; vector crop polygons rasterized into ground-truth masks (classification → segmentation).
- **Model:** Classical U-Net (Ronneberger 2015), 23 convolutional layers, symmetric encoder/decoder with skip connections, `1×1` final convolution. Implemented in PyTorch + FastAI.
- **Training:** Tiles cut into `64×64×8` patches; Adam optimizer, learning rate `1e-3`, **Dice loss**, batch size 16, 50 epochs (saturating ~epoch 43). Hardware: a single **GPU (NVIDIA T4, 12 GB) on Google Colab** — i.e., GPU-dependent, though modest.
- **Baselines compared:** Random Forest, CatBoost, KNN (classical ML); FCN and SegNet (deep segmentation).

**Evaluation protocol (load-bearing):** The dataset was tiled into `64×64` patches and split **80% / 10% / 10% into train / validation / test by patch**. This is an **in-region patch split, not a field-disjoint split**: there is **no demonstration that fields are disjoint across train/test**, and within a single region patches from the same field — or spatially adjacent, autocorrelated pixels — can land on both sides. On the spectrum our manuscript is built around (pooled-pixel k-fold → field-wise FID k-fold within one scene → spatial holdout → cross-domain transfer), this sits at the **pooled/random-patch within-one-region rung — *below* our field-wise grouped-CV bar.** The headline "test-set" accuracy is therefore an in-region, potentially field-leaky estimate, not a measure of spatial transfer. (The paper itself acknowledges, in its conclusion, that region-calibrated crop detectors "cannot be readily extended to another" region and lists cross-site transfer only as *future* work — confirming it did not test transfer.)

## Key Findings

- **Headline (overall, on the patch test split):** U-Net achieved **overall accuracy 95.3%, precision 80.2%, recall 68.1%, F1 73.6%** (Table 2). U-Net was the best model on F1; the deep segmentation baselines followed (FCN F1 69.26%, SegNet 63.33%), and all deep models beat classical ML.
- **The overall accuracy massively masks per-class performance.** A 95.3% overall accuracy coexists with a 73.6% F1 and a 68.1% recall — and far worse minority performance — because the metric is dominated by the easy majority/background pixels. This is a textbook class-imbalance artifact: *do not read 95.3% as crop-discrimination skill.*
- **Random Forest collapses on unengineered inputs.** The RF baseline scored only **F1 31.35%** (accuracy 50.18%, precision 34.4%, recall 28.8%) — the worst of all six models. Crucially, this RF was fed the raw/interpolated band-and-`NDVI` inputs used by the segmentation pipeline, **without engineered phenological time-series features**. CatBoost (F1 42.74%) and KNN (F1 48.89%) also trailed badly.
- **Per-class accuracy (Table 3) — single crops modest, intercrops collapse.** Single crops: common bean **70.4%**, maize **69.1%**, cassava **64.0%**. Intercropped classes fall to **24.9%** (maize+common bean), **22.0%** (maize+cassava), **19.4%** (maize+soybean), **15.6%** (cassava+common bean). (These per-class figures are reported as **accuracy**, not F1.) The intercrop classes — the structurally hardest, minority-style classes — are barely learned even by the best model.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

- **Closest like-for-like comparator we have for the deep-learning side.** Same continent-region tier (East African Global South, Kenya next door to Tanzania), same sensor and resolution (Sentinel-2 10 m), same smallholder/sparse-label/cloud regime, and overlapping crops (maize, cassava). It is the natural foil for the "lite learning vs. deep learning" argument.
- **Their RF-at-31.35%-F1 is the headline cautionary tale for our thesis — but read it carefully.** Their Random Forest failed not because tree-based ML is weak, but because it was fed **raw/interpolated bands + `NDVI` with no engineered time-series features**. Our pipeline does the opposite: `xr_fresh` tsfresh-style statistics (mean, max, min, slope, skewness, number of peaks, complexity) on `EVI`, `B2`, `B6`, `B11`/`B12`, `hue` *before* the tree model. This paper is strong empirical evidence that **the feature engineering, not the GPU/architecture, is what makes classical ML competitive** — exactly our claim. Frame our Kappa 0.82 / F1-micro 0.85 against their RF F1 31.35% as a feature-engineering result, not a model-class result.
- **Their intercrop collapse (15.6–24.9%) mirrors our minority-crop difficulty** and corroborates the Bégué-review verdict that optical RS struggles with intercropping at medium resolution. Several of our hard minority crops (cassava, millet, sunflower, sorghum, cotton) are grown intercropped in smallholder Tanzania, so this paper quantifies the structural ceiling we are pushing against and validates that minority/mixed classes are *the* hard problem — not a quirk of our data.
- **Evaluation-rigor contrast in our favor.** They use a patch-based 80/10/10 split with no shown field-disjointness and report overall accuracy prominently. We use `StratifiedGroupKFold` on `field_id` with `Field_size` weighting and lead with Cohen's Kappa / per-class F1. We can explicitly note that their 95.3% overall accuracy is an in-region, patch-split figure that is **not comparable** to our field-grouped-CV Kappa, and that our protocol is designed to avoid exactly the field-leakage their split does not rule out.
- **Cost contrast.** Their pipeline needs a GPU (T4) and a deep encoder-decoder; ours is GPU-free feature extraction + LightGBM/RF. Even granting their better headline number on their own (leakier) protocol, the cost/interpretability asymmetry is central to our "lite" framing.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **In-region patch split disguised as "test-set" accuracy.** The 80/10/10 split is by `64×64` patch within one region; field-disjointness is not demonstrated, so same-field/adjacent-pixel leakage between train and test is unguarded. Treat all their figures as **below our field-grouped-CV bar** and as **not directly comparable** to our reported Kappa/F1.
- **Overall accuracy is class-imbalance-inflated.** 95.3% OA vs. 73.6% F1 vs. ~16–25% intercrop accuracy is the classic gap; cite the F1 / per-class numbers (and the protocol), never the 95.3%.
- **Per-class metric is accuracy, not F1.** Table 3's per-class numbers (e.g. cassava 64.0%, intercrops 15.6–24.9%) are reported as *accuracy*; do not relabel them as per-class F1 when comparing to our per-class F1.
- **No spatial / cross-year / cross-sensor transfer.** Single growing season, single region; the authors concede transfer is untested and list it as future work. So this clears none of the higher rungs (it does *not* outrank our bar; it sits one rung below it).
- **No Cohen's Kappa, no macro-F1 reported.** They give overall accuracy/precision/recall/F1 and per-class accuracy, but no balanced aggregate (Kappa or macro-F1) — limiting a clean apples-to-apples minority-aware comparison with our metrics.
- **Confusing self-comparison wording.** The paper twice states U-Net is "lagging the best competitor, i.e., FCN, by 4.34% on F1," yet its own Table 2 shows U-Net F1 73.6% *above* FCN 69.26% (a 4.34-point *lead*). The table is the reliable source: U-Net is their top model.
- **GPU-dependent.** Requires CUDA (T4); not a CPU-only / edge-deployable method — relevant when contrasting computational cost with our GPU-free approach.
