# Sentinel SAR‑optical fusion for improving in‑season wheat crop mapping at a large scale using machine learning and the Google Earth engine platform

**Citation:** Zoungrana, L. E., Barbouchi, M., Toukabri, W., Babasy, M. O., Khatra, N. B., Annabi, M., & Bahri, H. (2024). Sentinel SAR-optical fusion for improving in-season wheat crop mapping at a large scale using machine learning and the Google Earth engine platform. *Applied Geomatics*, 16(1), 147–160. DOI: `10.1007/s12518-023-00545-4` (verified against Crossref — title, authors, journal, and year all match).

## Objectives

Map in-season wheat growing area at a regional scale in the Governorate of Jendouba, north-western Tunisia (~3102 km²), and quantify how much SAR–optical fusion improves accuracy over single-sensor classification. The authors run three parallel experiments — Sentinel-1 (`S1`) only, Sentinel-2 (`S2`) only, and fused `S1 + S2` — to isolate the marginal value of radar. A secondary, explicitly stated goal is a method "both accurate and simple enough to be easily adopted and further developed by agricultural technical institutions," i.e. a low-complexity, classical-ML, cloud-platform pipeline — the same lightweight, institution-friendly framing as our "Lite Learning" study.

## Methods

- **Sensors / features.** Six monthly Sentinel-1 IW-GRD images (Dec 2021–May 2022), dual-polarization `VV`/`VH` at 10 m, Lee-filtered for speckle. Six cloud-free Sentinel-2 Level-2A BOA images over the same window; five bands (`B2`, `B3`, `B4`, `B8`, `B11`) plus `B12` (resampled 20 m → 10 m), and two vegetation indices, `NDVI` and `MSAVI`. Inputs are stacked multi-temporal composites — **raw band/index time stacks**, not engineered tsfresh-style temporal statistics. There is no per-pixel temporal feature extraction (mean/slope/skewness/peaks) of the kind our `xr_fresh` pipeline computes; phenology enters only through the six monthly layers and through manual `NDVI`/`MSAVI` thresholding to separate wheat from barley.
- **Classifier.** Random Forest (300 trees, `max_features` = sqrt) run entirely inside Google Earth Engine for pixel-level prediction. Variable importance via Gini decrease. A **two-step hierarchical classification**: step 1 separates a generic crop class from natural vegetation, water, and built-up; step 2 splits crops into seasonal vs perennial and isolates wheat. This generic "crop vs not-crop" first stage is conceptually the same coarse "agriculture" label that the Kerner et al. (2024) Sub-Saharan benchmark critiques and that our study aims to beat with crop-specific classes.
- **Labels.** 2880 field samples from a random GPS/photo field survey (7–20 March 2022) covering wheat plus a long tail of other crops and three non-crop classes (natural vegetation, water, urban). Artificially balanced design: 720 per main type (504 train / 216 validation each).
- **Evaluation protocol (load-bearing).** A single **random 70% train / 30% validation split of the reference sample points**, with error matrices computed per experiment (A/B/C). Crucially, **there is no field-level (FID) grouping and no spatial separation between train and test** — points are split randomly across the whole sample pool. The paper labels its 30% as "validation"/accuracy assessment, but on the manuscript's spectrum this sits at the **lowest rung: pooled/random split over all sample points within a single scene/governorate**, *below* our field-wise grouped CV. This is exactly the in-region-random-split-disguised-as-out-of-sample conflation that our `StratifiedGroupKFold` on `field_id` is designed to avoid. No cross-season, cross-region, or spatial-holdout transfer is tested; the authors themselves list "results could not be validated on different cropping seasons due to lack of ground sample data" as a known shortcoming.

## Key Findings

- **Fusion wins, modestly.** Overall accuracy / Kappa by experiment: `S1` only 89.88% / 0.75; `S2` only 94.35% / 0.86; `S1 + S2` fused 95.88% / 0.90. Fusion adds **~6% OA over `S1`-only and ~1.5% (1.53%) over `S2`-only** — the headline "1.5 to 6%" SAR-optical gain. The dominant single sensor is optical; radar's marginal contribution over good optical data is small (~1.5%).
- **Wheat-specific accuracy** (from survey samples): producer 84%, user 81%, F1 0.82, with a wheat-step Kappa of 0.77. These wheat figures are lower than the 95.88% land-cover OA — a reminder that the high headline number is a 4-class land-cover problem (crop / natural veg / built-up / water), not crop-type discrimination.
- **SWIR carries the optical signal.** `S2`-only already reaches 0.86 Kappa using `B11`/`B12` SWIR plus red/NIR indices; the SWIR bands are explicitly resampled in to be retained. Corroborates our reliance on `B11`/`B12`.
- **Per-class weakness is visible in the confusion matrix.** Built-up F1 is poor in `S1` (0.57) and `S2` (0.55), rising to 0.82 only with fusion; the minority/hard class is carried by radar. Cropland and water are easy (F1 0.93–0.98 throughout).
- **Wheat–barley separability is hard and handled by hand-tuned thresholds**, not by the classifier — `NDVI`/`MSAVI` curves overlap substantially ("a high degree of overlap"). This is the cereal-confusion problem analogous to our maize-vs-other-cereal (sorghum/millet) difficulty.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This is the closest **classical-ML cousin** to our pipeline in the corpus: multi-temporal Sentinel imagery, Random Forest, run on a cloud platform (GEE), with an explicit "simple enough for technical institutions" design philosophy that mirrors our "lite learning" thesis. It is a useful *positive* precedent that tree-based ML on Sentinel features can reach high accuracy without deep learning, and it independently validates our **band choices** (`B11`/`B12` SWIR retained at cost; red/NIR indices; `S2` carrying most of the signal).

But it is a **weaker comparator on every rigor axis our paper is built around**, and should be cited as such:

1. **Evaluation rigor (the central point).** Their 95.88% OA / 0.90 Kappa comes from a **random 70/30 split over pooled sample points with no field grouping and no spatial holdout**. Spatially autocorrelated pixels from the same field can land in both train and test, inflating accuracy without measuring transfer. Our Cohen's Kappa 0.82 / F1-micro 0.85 is measured under **field-disjoint `StratifiedGroupKFold`** — a strictly harder bar. The two numbers are **not directly comparable**, and their higher headline is partly an artifact of the easier protocol and the easier 4-class land-cover task. This paper is the textbook example to cite when we argue that field-grouped CV is necessary and that random-split accuracies in the literature are optimistic / leakage-prone.
2. **SAR-optical fusion gain we forgo.** They quantify the cost of being optical-only: ~1.5% OA below fused `S1 + S2` (and ~6% above `S1`-only). Our study is **optical-only by design** (`EVI`, `B2`, `B6`, `B11`, `B12`, `hue`), so this gives a concrete, citable estimate of the small accuracy we trade away for a simpler, single-sensor pipeline that handles clouds through temporal feature engineering rather than radar — a worthwhile trade in a smallholder, data-scarce setting where the ~1.5% optical→fusion gain is dwarfed by the optimism in their leakage-prone evaluation.
3. **Feature representation contrast.** They use raw monthly composites plus manual index thresholds for the hard wheat-vs-barley case; we replace hand-tuned thresholds and raw stacks with **automatically extracted tsfresh-style temporal statistics** fed to gradient-boosted trees and explained with SHAP. Their reliance on manual `NDVI`/`MSAVI` thresholding to separate two cereals is precisely the brittleness our learned temporal features remove.
4. **Region / sensor.** North-African (Tunisian) winter wheat under uni-modal rainfall and relatively cloud-free conditions — an *easier* optical environment than our cloud-heavy northern-Tanzania Masika season, which further inflates their `S2`-only numbers relative to what the same method would yield in our setting.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **Spatial leakage (unaddressed).** Random 70/30 over pooled points; no FID-disjoint split, no spatial separation of train/test. Reported accuracy almost certainly overstates true transferable performance. Flag whenever this paper is used as a benchmark — it sits *below* our field-wise grouped-CV bar.
- **Headline metric mixes tasks.** The 95.88% OA / 0.90 Kappa is a **4-class land-cover** result (crop / natural veg / built-up / water); the actual crop-type (wheat) discrimination is the weaker F1 0.82 / Kappa 0.77, and even that leans on hand-built phenology thresholds rather than the classifier.
- **Class-imbalance / minority handling.** Sample design is artificially balanced (720 per main type), so reported metrics do not reflect operational class priors; built-up is visibly weak (F1 0.55–0.57) until fusion. No macro-F1 or per-class Kappa across a full crop tail is reported — wheat is the only crop reported individually; "other seasonal crops" (barley, oats, maize, etc.) are pooled, so true per-minority-crop performance is unmeasured.
- **No cross-season / cross-region transfer**, explicitly flagged by the authors as a limitation.
- **Easier optical regime** (sub-humid Tunisia, six cloud-free `S2` scenes obtainable) than our cloudy smallholder East-African setting — limits transfer of their accuracy claims to our context.
- **Computational cost not reported**, but the method is GEE-native and lightweight (RF, 300 trees), consistent with the "lite," institution-deployable framing both papers share.
