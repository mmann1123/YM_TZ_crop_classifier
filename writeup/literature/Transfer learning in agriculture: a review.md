# Transfer learning in agriculture: a review

**Citation:** Hossen, M. I., Awrangjeb, M., Pan, S., & Al Mamun, A. (2025). Transfer learning in agriculture: a review. *Artificial Intelligence Review*, 58, Article 97. DOI: `10.1007/s10462-024-11081-x` (verified against Crossref — title, authors, journal, and year all match). Accepted 13 Dec 2024; published online 25 Jan 2025.

> **Scope note (rule 7):** This is a **systematic review of transfer learning (TL)**, not a crop-type-classification study. There is no single evaluation protocol to calibrate against our field-disjoint grouped-CV bar, so the "Relevance" section focuses on conceptual/methodological implications — the deep-learning-vs-classical-ML contrast, data scarcity, compute, generalization, interpretability — and the "Evaluation Caveats" section assesses the review's rigor as a review.

## Objectives

Provide the first dedicated systematic review (PRISMA methodology) of **transfer learning in agriculture**, scoped to seven plant-centric tasks: plant-species recognition, plant-disease recognition, crop–weed detection, seedling detection, pest recognition, plant-nutrient-deficiency detection, and plant-growth prediction. Five research questions catalogue (RQ1) which CNN backbones are used, (RQ2) dataset sources, (RQ3) input image types, (RQ4) implementation platforms, and (RQ5) TL category. The review then distills the **challenges** of applying TL in agriculture and recommends future directions. It is, in effect, a complete tour of the **ImageNet-pretrained-CNN pipeline that our "Lite Learning" study deliberately avoids** — making it the ideal deep-learning *foil* for our argument.

## Methods (of the review)

- **PRISMA-based** literature selection and analysis structured around the Goal-Question-Metric formulation; findings synthesized by RQ.
- **What the surveyed field actually does (the pipeline we contrast against):** a CNN backbone (overwhelmingly **VGG, ResNet, AlexNet**) is pre-trained on a large general-purpose computer-vision corpus — almost always **ImageNet** (and COCO) — then adapted to a small agricultural target dataset. **Model-based TL (fine-tuning)** is the dominant approach; inputs are mostly **RGB** (some hyperspectral); implementation is mostly **TensorFlow/PyTorch** (i.e. GPU deep-learning stacks). TL is taxonomized into model-/instance-/relational-/feature-based categories (Pan & Yang).
- **Stated motivation for the whole TL enterprise** is exactly the constraint our study also faces: "progress is constrained by the lack of large labelled datasets" because annotation is "time-consuming, labour-intensive, and requires expert knowledge." TL is offered as *the* deep-learning answer to data scarcity. Our study answers the same scarcity differently — with engineered features and tree models that need far less data and no pretraining.

## Key Findings (the eight challenges — the load-bearing content for us)

The review's Section 5.6 enumerates the recurring failure modes of TL/deep learning in agriculture; these are precisely the costs that argue *for* an interpretable, grouped-CV, classical-ML alternative:

1. **Poor cross-dataset generalization.** A flagship example: a TL method scored **97.4% on the PlantVillage dataset but only 63.8% on the IVADL-Tomato dataset** — a ~34-point collapse when the test data shift. The review notes many studies are validated only on their own data and need field validation to confirm real-world generalizability.
2. **Negative transfer.** ImageNet/COCO-pretrained convolutional features (low-level → high-level) are not tailored to agriculture, so transferring them can *hurt* the target task ("potentially leading to negative transfer"). Domain-specific pretraining is largely unavailable.
3. **Class imbalance.** Agricultural datasets are heavily imbalanced; the review cites a case where **TL with ResNet50 dropped by ~30%** due to overfitting caused by an imbalanced dataset, with small classes most affected and generalization restricted. (This is exactly the minority-crop problem — cassava, millet, sunflower, sorghum, cotton — that our balanced metrics and field-weighting target.)
4. **Model opacity / interpretability.** "TL models are often regarded as black boxes"; the lack of transparency makes it hard to understand why predictions are made and what to transfer — requiring scarce domain expertise.
5. **Fine-grained identification** of small or visually similar species/diseases/pests in complex backgrounds remains hard.
6. **Bridging the domain gap** between generic CV pretraining and agriculture is unsolved; the review calls for domain-specific, generalized pretrained models and for **few-shot / zero-shot** learning to cope with minimal labels.
7. **Heavy data and compute prerequisites** — CNNs "demonstrate generalisability and improved results on an adequately large dataset" and "performance heavily drops when there is a lack of enough labelled data," and training from scratch is time-consuming; TL mitigates but does not remove these costs (still GPU/TensorFlow/PyTorch pipelines).
8. **Satellite / remote-sensing TL is immature.** The review states that TL approaches for agricultural domains in real-world conditions "are still in their **infancy**," and explicitly notes that the surveyed field is dominated by **RGB proximal/UAV imagery**, with satellite remote sensing under-represented — i.e. the deep-TL evidence base barely covers our exact problem (multi-temporal satellite crop typing).

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This review is the **ideal deep-learning foil** for the manuscript: it lays out, in the field's own words, the full ImageNet-pretrained-CNN/fine-tuning pipeline and then catalogues why that pipeline struggles in agriculture — and every one of its eight challenges is an argument *for* our interpretable, grouped-CV, feature-based classical-ML approach.

1. **Generalization collapse (97.4% → 63.8%) is the deep-learning version of the very leakage problem our evaluation guards against.** When a CNN's headline accuracy falls 34 points on a shifted dataset, it shows that impressive in-distribution numbers do not survive distribution shift — exactly why we report **field-disjoint `StratifiedGroupKFold`** rather than optimistic pooled-pixel accuracy. We can cite this as evidence that heavy models do not automatically generalize, and that honest evaluation matters more than headline numbers.
2. **Negative transfer & the domain gap.** ImageNet features are not built for multi-temporal Sentinel reflectance, and transferring them can hurt. Our engineered tsfresh-style temporal features (`EVI`, `B2`, `B6`, `B11`, `B12`, `hue`) are **purpose-built for the phenological signal**, sidestepping the negative-transfer risk entirely and needing no pretraining corpus.
3. **Class imbalance (~30% drops).** The review confirms that deep TL degrades sharply on minority classes — the same hard-minority-crop challenge (cassava, millet, sunflower, sorghum, cotton) we address with **Cohen's Kappa, per-class F1, and `Field_size` weighting**. Our balanced metrics directly answer a documented deep-learning weakness.
4. **Opacity vs interpretability.** The review names black-box opacity as a core unsolved TL problem. Our **SHAP-based feature importance** over interpretable temporal statistics is the direct remedy — we can explain *which* band/statistic/month drives each class decision, which fine-tuned CNNs cannot.
5. **Data & compute scarcity.** The review's entire premise is that agriculture lacks large labelled datasets and that CNNs are data- and compute-hungry. Our **GPU-free, low-data, crowdsourced-label (YouthMappers)** pipeline is a different and arguably better answer to the same constraint than ImageNet-transfer — no pretraining, no large GPU stack, fewer labels.
6. **"Satellite TL is in its infancy."** This is a citable admission that the deep-TL literature barely covers multi-temporal satellite crop typing in data-scarce regions — i.e. there is **no mature deep-learning incumbent** to beat in our exact niche, strengthening the case for a well-validated classical-ML method as the sensible state of the art for smallholder Sub-Saharan crop classification.

## Evaluation Caveats (as a review)

- **It is a review, not a benchmark** — no protocol, no accuracies of its own, nothing to calibrate against our field-grouped-CV bar; the per-study numbers it cites (97.4%/63.8%, the 30% imbalance drop) come from the reviewed papers and inherit *their* (often unstated or random-split, leakage-prone) evaluation protocols. Treat those figures as illustrative of failure modes, not as rigorous benchmarks.
- **Scope is plant-centric / proximal imagery**, dominated by RGB UAV and leaf-scale datasets across seven non-crop-typing tasks (disease, weed, seedling, pest, nutrient, growth, species). It does **not** cover multi-temporal satellite crop-type classification, field-grouped evaluation, or spatial-transfer rigor — so it informs the deep-learning-vs-feature-engineering *argument* but offers no direct accuracy comparator for our task.
- **The authors disclose review biases** (selection, publication, generalization, interpretation bias) and a refined scope, which appropriately limits the strength of its synthesized claims.
- **No quantitative head-to-head with classical ML** is presented; the contrast we draw (engineered features + trees vs pretrained CNNs) is built *from* the review's catalogued weaknesses, not from a controlled comparison it performs.
